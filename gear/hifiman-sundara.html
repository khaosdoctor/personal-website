Description #

Open-back planar magnetic headphones from HIFIMAN, launched in February 2018 with a diaphragm HIFIMAN says is 80% thinner than the one in the HE400 series. In practice, I haven't really noticed anything different, the Sundara is quieter, but people say it's not because it's harder to drive, but because the initial response is below the average input volume.

I mainly bought this to test a planar magnetic headphone which was a new thing to me. For what I have been seeing, it's has quite remarkable Frequency Response chart:

Soundguys says it "follows our studio curve exceptionally well" and that they "may even add its frequency response to the corpus of benchmark products for our next iteration of the curve. It’s just that good.", so I guess it has some quite interesting sound.

Impressions #

Initially, the box was super well packed, and quite fancy, with some clothing there to make it luxury. But the overall construction of the headphone is pretty solid. The whole thing is metal, even the cups, only the cushions are not.

There's a leather strip inside to make a suspension band, which I like a lot for two reasons:

  1. It's easier to store the headphone without bending it
  2. It's more comfortable

The headphone itself is quite heavy. I think it weights like 400g, but it's surprisingly comfortable to use, the only thing that I notice is that my ears are a bit large to fit the cups, so maybe getting different cups would be the solution?

Over all, it's a very solid headphone. The drivers do have some interesting quirks. Planar magnetic drives are usually bigger because, different from their dynamic driver counterparts, they need quite big magnets, so the whole thing is bigger (the cups are huge) but they're, in theory, brighter and more defined as the diaphragm can move faster and stop faster as well.

This is quite true, I can clearly notice that Mids are more defined, especially on the voice level and the high mids close to the "attack" range. So snares and cymbals usually also sound better. And the most amazing thing is that some songs I couldn't tell what the artist was singing are now super clear, this was my "aha!" moment.

A good thing is that the cable is detachable, and each cup has one 3.5mm jack, which is a good thing because I can just replace the cable, which is, by far, the worst part of this headphone. It is super wonky and looks cheap. Other than that it's all good.

The fact that it also doesn't fold is a bit annoying. But on the sound, compared to other open backs like the Beyerdynamics DT 990 Pro and the Lyrö, I noticed it is brighter, the sound is more "filled", even though I did EQ it

Final thoughts #

It's a very good headphone, probably the sturdiest I have. But I don't think the price is justified, the planar drives are really good and they really showcase interesting things in the music, but at this price I think there are better ones with dynamic drives. I got this a second hand with a very good price, so it was ok, but I wouldn't buy a new one for the full price, I would probably get the HE400se even though it looks more "plastic".
